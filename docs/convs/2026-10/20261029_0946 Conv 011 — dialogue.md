> The three phases to software Dev in the AI Age, as I see it
> Straw man phase
> you have an idea for your business or organization that you have been wanting to do
> it could be a desktop app, web app, mobile app or a browser extension
> you've heard the buzz about AI replacing programmers and enabling anyone to write an app (and it's true)
> you decide to try AI, and together develop a plan to get it done
> you then let the AI write the app for you
> But, what AI created for you isn't what you expected
> it's missing some things
> it has a clunky way of doing a few aspects
> it really got a few thing right, though, and it looks amazing
> you tinker with what the AI produced, but you cannot get it right
> its missing very importnat details
> there are things you should have told it to make it better
> you realize that the AI is making many decisions for you, and they are often not the ones you wanted
> many people stop here disillusioned (hint: I can help here)
> a few people take what they learned and move on to the Tin Man
> Tin man phase
> first, you isolate the straw man coding and direction, plan. You don't build over top of the straw man. The tin man is a new effort that learns from the straw man and can access its code and learn from it
> You take what you learned from the straw man, and you make some hard choices
> you separate out for a future "Phase 2" some overly ambitious goals and features
> you redefine what it is you want, you list features by priority
> you note sequencing of features, and interactions that now are obvious to you
> you shore up the loose instructions you gave the straw man with details you left out and ideally examples
> this is a very interactive phase where you work with the AI. It asks you questions you answer
> together, you rewrite the new plan
> then you let the AI build it again
> this app takes much longer to build
> you find it runs out of context (limited tokens it keeps track of in a session) and you have to manage its bursts of work over multiple sessions
> you have to remember where you are in the plan and keep track of what you see that might need to be adjusted
> eventually, the app is built anew and it is much better, but not perfect
> there are more things that you would change, but the way in which that would work within what is already built is not obvious
> it is here that the UI, particularly the menu structure where you move around your app seems is desperate need of a revision
> you toy with the idea of living with its present feature set and ways of doing work
> you wonder if this is usable by your team
> you try it for a few days and decide this is ok for my use and you continue to tinker
> if the app is just for you, this is probably the stage you stop. You keep tinkering and you use it yourself
> if the app is made for your team, or outside users, you know it has to be made more rigorous, users, security, deployment all rise up and ask to be considered
> you realize that these areas you don't have confidence about and are not even sure how to instruct the AI
> if you go on, then you enter the Steel man phase
> Steel man phase
> once again, you isolate the Tin man code and planning and start fresh, knowing that you can access the code and planning of both the tin and straw men as needed
> at this point you really organize your plan for the app. You take the tin man plan and you organize it into sections, and you add new sections for things like security, server api, remote apps you might need (Stripe, Mail, Slack, error monitoring, database, deployment, version control, etc)
> you identify which sections need your input and which need outside input
> you ask the AI to fill in the sections you identified as outside of your knowledge
> your app just became huge, with many moving pieces and you can start to feel that if you let AI develop according to this new plan, that you will have no idea if the sections you had it create are working
> it is at this point you might appreciate a little assistance.
> if you got this far, i am impressed. You are are the perfect client for me and we will work well together
> you have determination, a buildable project (vetted by you as useful) and you are familiar with AI now - with what is can do and what it can't do
> AI can build what you say to the extent it understands the goal
> AI builds much better software when it interacts with you and you have the knowledge and instincts to decide its course, especially when it veers off course
