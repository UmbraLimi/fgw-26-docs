## Core stance

- **Seasoned — 40+ years — but subtle about it.** State the experience *once*, in its place; never
  hammer it. The seasoning should be **felt by the reader, not repeated at them**. Years are a quiet
  background fact, not a headline drumbeat.
- **Humble, but not too humble.** The impression to leave: good, seasoned, genuinely helpful. Never
  above the reader or above others who do this work (reinforces the `VOICE.md` humility rule).
  Confidence without boast.
- **Doesn't need the work.** Selective — bandwidth for only **1–2 projects at a time**. This reads as
  independence and quality, never as scarcity-marketing. Never desperate, never hungry, never chasing.

## What he actually offers

- **Wisdom about problem-solving approaches — that AI greatly amplifies.** The offer is not "I write
  code." It's a *way of thinking about problems*, now multiplied by AI. That wisdom is the product; the
  code is the output.
- **Helping you become a worthy companion to AI.** The tool is powerful — magical, even — but it fills in
  missing detail on its own, and it can only steer by what you give it: your prompts, your context, your
  history with it. The gap to close isn't AI's *reliability* — it's the human skill of directing it, so it
  builds what you actually mean. 🗝️ **"A worthy companion to AI"** (operator's phrase, Conv 036) — a
  likely through-line for content.
- **A spectrum of involvement, not a menu.** Per the site (`/phases`): "a hand to guide the whole thing,
  fix one stubborn problem, or take a project over" — he meets the client wherever they are on the
  straw/tin/steel arc, rather than selling fixed packages.

## Audience — who he educates, and about what

- **Primary audience: buyers, not builders-for-hire.** Business owners, managers, and operators who need
  software and are now commissioning or buying AI-built apps — and are rightly wary of what AI produces.
  **Not** professional programmers serving *their own* clients (considered and set aside Conv 036:
  teaching peers builds reputation, not pipeline, and breaks the funnel — LinkedIn exists to bring people
  to the site, which sells to business owners).
- **The buyer/coder line is blurred; the real divider is vernacular, not concept (operator, Conv 040).**
  Fraser's buyers have often tried to build with AI themselves, so the *concepts* aren't foreign to them —
  what separates buyer copy from coder copy is the **vernacular**: coder jargon like "logging" or "edge
  cases." Keep buyer-facing copy in plain language; use a technical term only when deliberately introduced
  and explained (e.g. "edge cases" is kept and defined in the Trust guide, while "logging" was cut). This
  is where the drift-watch bites: reframe the *jargon*, not necessarily the idea.
- **What he educates about:** how to get AI-built software you can **trust** — how to judge whether it's
  sound, what AI gets confidently wrong, how to steer it, when to build-your-own vs. bring someone in.
  Craft wisdom translated into *buyer* terms.
- **How they see AI (the chord to strike).** *Not* "is AI reliable?" — that's an engineer's question.
  Their actual view: genuine **wonder** (it built something real from a sentence, and that amazement is
  earned, not hype-swallowed); then the **last-mile frustration** (almost-right, makes choices for them,
  can't steer or fix it); and underneath, a **trust-you-can't-verify unease** as it gets real — *is this
  sound, or does it just look like it?* Their real question is *"can I get something good and trustworthy
  out of it, or am I going to get burned?"* **Strike this chord by validating the wonder and naming the
  unease — without making AI scary (empowering, not fear).**
- **The audience spans the three self-qualification phases** (site `/phases` — straw-man → tin-man →
  steel-man), crucially **including those who have already attempted to build with AI themselves**. These
  are people building for *their own* needs, not programmers building for clients; he meets them wherever
  they are on the arc and is the seasoned judgement they call in:
  - **Straw man** — had AI build it; it came back almost-right but not quite, and they can't fix it; many
    stop here disillusioned. *(He reads what the AI built, says plainly what's inside, helps decide what's
    worth keeping before they start over.)*
  - **Tin man** — started fresh, a real planning back-and-forth, a better build; good enough they wonder
    if their team could use it — then real-users / security / deployment questions stand up. *(He shapes
    the plan, sets priorities and sequence, keeps the AI on course across sessions.)*
  - **Steel man** — organising it like a real product (security, a server API, outside services); it
    gets big and they can't verify the parts they don't understand. *(He takes the sections outside their
    expertise and makes sure they actually work, so nothing runs on faith.)*

## Outlook & propensities

- **Empowering, not gatekeeping.** Believes most people can now build their own tools with motivation +
  AI — "like the DIY home-repair industry: you fix it yourself with a good video, and when you hit
  something unusual, you know who to call." He is the one you call for the unusual. (His own words.)
- **Educator by nature.** Shares, encourages, teaches — warm, practical ("have a notebook handy; write
  down the ideas that spring up"). Sales-forward pitching is **against his nature** and should be avoided
  in anything written as him.
- **Teaches coders too, but that is a side channel (operator, Conv 040).** His educator instinct sometimes
  drifts into teaching fellow coders who use AI to build — a *builder* audience, not the *buyer* audience
  the funnel serves (see Audience). 🔎 **Watch for this drift in buyer-facing copy and reframe to the
  buyer.** The builder/buyer *difference* can itself be instructive to buyers, but the framing stays
  buyer-first. He does see a **rare, separate "for builders" article** as the proper home for genuinely
  coder-aimed advice (it showcases the teaching propensity); coder-framed material cut from buyer copy is
  preserved in `_cc/for-builders-tidbits.md` rather than dropped. (First catch: Trust guide edge-cases
  passage, Conv 040.)
- **Science-trained thinker.** Biology background (field + lab): *measure before you conclude, test the
  hypothesis, let the evidence decide.* Methodical with complex systems; evidence over assertion.
- **Intuition is the upstream of judgement (operator, Conv 040).** A major theme: help the reader build
  their *own* intuition, and use it as the trigger for judgement. The felt "something is off" is the signal
  to stop and look harder. He writes to surface and develop that instinct in people, never to override it.
- **Judgement is the throughline.** Across every tool-shift in 40+ years, what carried was knowing *what
  to build, what to refuse, and when something isn't right yet*. The programming language was never the
  interesting question.
- **Candid.** Names what went wrong; a documented miss is a trust signal, not a weakness to hide. Never a
  claim the evidence won't support.

## Implications for social + site work

- **Lead with usefulness / teaching, not credentials.** Let seasoning surface once and quietly.
- **Signal selectivity and independence, never hunger.** Soft CTAs; the posture is "the work comes to
  me," not "please hire me."
- **Demonstrate the thinking** (problem-solving approaches + how AI amplifies them) rather than advertise
  services.
- **Quiet-editorial restraint.** No hype, no superlatives, no badge-collecting.
- **Credibility by proof, not self-praise** — grounded specifics and others vouching (testimonials,
  recommendations), not assertions about himself.
