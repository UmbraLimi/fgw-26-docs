## Approved copy

### Headline
> I help business owners get software they can trust from AI — and learn to steer it themselves.

### About
> Most business owners don't need a programmer the way they used to. AI can write real software now — and if you've tried it, you've seen both sides: some of what comes back is genuinely impressive, and some is almost-right in ways you can't quite fix.
>
> That gap is where I work. I help business owners and operators get software they can actually trust out of AI — whether you want it built for you, want a hand with the part that's stuck, or want to learn to build it yourself.
>
> Here's the part that gets missed. When AI builds something real from a few plain sentences, the wonder is genuine — that really is new, and it really is that good. What snags you is everything it quietly decides on your behalf, and the harder question underneath: can you trust what it built when you can't see into the parts you don't understand? That's not you being out of your depth — it's the real shape of working with AI. And it's exactly where judgement earns its keep. The skill that matters isn't coding; it's learning to be a worthy companion to the AI — knowing what to tell it, what to push back on, and when something isn't right yet. That's most of what I bring.
>
> I came to software from science — degrees in biology, years of field and lab work — and the habit that stuck was the useful one: measure before you conclude, test the idea, let the evidence decide. I've built software through a lot of tool changes since the early 1980s; AI is the newest and biggest, and the same judgement carries across all of them.
>
> I take on only a project or two at a time, so most of what I do here is share how this actually works — the approaches that get AI to build the right thing. If that's useful, follow along. And if you've got something real you're trying to build, the rest of the story is at frasergorrie.com.
